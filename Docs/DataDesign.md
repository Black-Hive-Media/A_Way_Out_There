A Way Out There — Data & Economy Design
Nov 24, 2026 · @Mandy
Overview
A Way Out There is a casual, family-friendly, no-combat 2D top-down space salvage and trading game for 1–4 players in split-screen on PC, Switch and Xbox. Players tractor-beam debris, mine asteroids, sell and buy at space ports in a live economy, run deliveries, and upgrade their ship. This doc sets the data model and rules for that, built on the existing Unity 6 project.

Rules:
• Every record has a stable string ID (sys_sol, port_sol_refinery, itm_gold, upg_laser_t2). Save data refers to content only by ID, never by asset reference, so content can change without breaking saves.
• Save files carry a schema_version and run migrations on load.
• Saving goes through an ISaveStorage interface with one implementation per platform: PC (files under Application.persistentDataPath), Switch (its save-data file system), Xbox (GDK connected storage). Game code never knows the platform.
• Switch save space is limited, so the item history is bounded: full ledger rows only for items still owned; older events roll up into per-item totals (count sold, total earned, best price).
• Save on dock, undock, purchase and quit; never during a frame-critical moment.

Economy simulation
Every port keeps a stock level per item, and its price comes from that stock against a target level, clamped between a floor and a ceiling. Industries make some ports produce what others consume, so trade routes appear without scripting.
Price
The mid price of an item at a port, with r = stock ÷ target_stock and e = the curve's elasticity:
\text{mid} = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\text{base\_value} \times \text{variant\_mult} \times \text{port\_mult} \times r^{-e},\ \text{min\_price},\ \text{max\_price}\right)
• The port sells to players at mid × (1 + spread) and buys from players at mid × (1 − spread). The spread stops instant buy-and-resell profit at one port.
• Price is recalculated after every unit, so selling 10 Gold at once pays a little less for each one.
• min_price is the floor: a player always gets something for what they haul, even when the port is past max_stock.
• Between ticks, a displayed price may move at most max_price_step, so prices drift smoothly.
Buyback rule
An item sold back to the port it was bought at pays the lower of the normal buy price and the player's purchase price minus the buyback discount. The rule never expires: it applies to that item at that port for as long as it exists:
\text{sell\_price} = \min\left(\text{port\_buy\_price},\ \text{purchase\_price} \times (1 - \text{buyback\_discount})\right)
The purchase port and price live on the ItemInstance, so the rule follows the item through gifts and ship-to-ship trades. Any other port pays its normal price.
Simulation tick
The economy runs on a fixed tick in sim time, separate from frame rate, seeded from Universe.seed so runs are reproducible. Each tick:
1.  Add production and remove consumption for every PortMarket row, scaled by tick length; clamp stock to 0…max_stock.
2.  Advance NPC traders: arrivals trade or complete deliveries, then pick their next route or delivery.
3.  Recalculate mid prices with the step limit.
4.  Expire perishables, turning each into its low-value perished_item_id, and check delivery bonus windows.
The tick pauses with the game. Systems with no player in them run the same tick; their NPCs are only timers there, with no physics or sprites, to keep Switch frame rate safe.
NPC traders
On arrival at a port, an NPC sells what pays more than its min_profit_margin over what it paid, then scans known ports for the item with the best price gap it can afford and carry. It buys within its caps, sets a destination and an arrive_at from distance ÷ speed, and leaves. An NPC becomes a visible ship only in a system a player is in, where it can also request docking with players.

Docking and ship-to-ship trading
Ships dock by request and pilot approval, then see each other's cargo and asking prices and trade item by item, each trade approved by the receiving pilot. NPC traders use the same flow. In split-screen this is in-game comms, not networking, but the records are shaped so it could run online later.
Flow
1.  A pilot (or NPC) sends a dock request to a ship within range; both ships must be below the docking speed.
2.  The target pilot approves or declines. Declined ends it; an NPC answers from its personality.
3.  While docked, each ship shows its visible cargo with asking prices (defaulting to the current local port price).
4.  Either pilot proposes an offer: Buy, Sell or Gift, of items, fuel or credits.
5.  The receiving pilot accepts, declines or counters. Accepted moves items and credits between the two ships' cargo and wallets.
6.  Docking ends when either pilot undocks or either ship moves out of range.

Rules
• Ship-to-ship trades do not change port markets or stock.
• Every transfer writes an ItemLedger row (Traded or Gifted), so history shows who it came from.
• A fuel gift cannot take the giver below fuel_min_reserve.
• Cargo capacity is checked on the receiving ship before an offer can be accepted.
• NPC traders accept player offers only above their min_profit_margin and within their caps.
• Chat is preset quick-chat lines and emotes, not typed text: controller-friendly, family-safe, and it avoids the text-filtering and parental-control requirements Xbox and Nintendo apply to free text between players.
Upgrades, damage and repairs
Buying an upgrade installs it at once and trades in the old part for a refund based on what was paid and its condition. Parts wear from collisions and overheating, lose a little performance as they wear, and are repaired at ports.
Trade-in
\text{trade\_in} = \text{trade\_in\_basis} \times \text{trade\_in\_rate} \times \text{condition}
• For a bought part, trade_in_basis is what the crew paid. Example: a laser bought for 1,000 at 80% condition with a 60% rate returns 480 credits.
• Hull starting parts have a trade_in_basis of the hull's listed part value.
• The traded-in part is gone; it does not enter port stock.
Salvaged parts
ShipPart items found in debris (Landing Gear and Warp Drive today) point at an UpgradeDefinition through upgrade_id. A crew can sell one at market like any item, or install it at a port with an Upgrade service. Installing is free; its condition comes from the variant's condition_range. Its trade_in_basis is the upgrade's list cost × salvage_basis_rate, because nothing was paid for it.
Damage
\text{damage} = \text{base\_damage} \times \text{scale} \times (1 - \text{damage\_resistance})
• scale is impact speed for collisions and seconds spent overheated for the laser and tractor beam.
• Collisions damage the hull and, at a lower rate, a random installed part; overheating damages only that beam.
• Nothing is ever destroyed: condition stops at 0%, and the ship still flies.
• The ship AI warns at 50% and 25% condition through existing AI_Manager triggers.
Performance loss
Each stat scales between performance_floor and 100% with condition, so the loss stays minor:
\text{effective\_stat} = \text{base\_stat} \times \left(\text{floor} + (1 - \text{floor}) \times \text{condition}\right)
With a floor of 0.85, a part at 0% condition still works at 85%. Hull condition lowers top speed the same way.
Repairs
Repair cost = percent restored × cost_per_percent for that slot and tier × the port's multiplier. Crews can repair partly to fit their credits. Each repair writes a ShipServiceLog row.

Community goals
Two or three community goals always run at once, each a large delivery target for one system that every player adds to on their own schedule and sees progress on. Each lasts 4–7 real-time days with staggered deadlines. Contributors are paid per hand-in straight away; the big bonus pays only if the target is met before the deadline.
Scheduler rules
• Never fewer than 2 active goals, never more than 3. When a goal ends (met or expired), a replacement starts at once if fewer than 2 remain.
• Duration is 4–7 days on the real-time clock, never shorter. Goals use real time even though the economy pauses, so they keep moving between sessions.
• Deadlines stagger: every active deadline is at least 12 hours from every other. With a 72-hour window and at most two other deadlines, a valid end time always exists. (A 24-hour gap can fail: two deadlines can block the whole window.)
• No two active goals in the same system, and no theme twice in a row.
• A goal met early shows its success story for 24 hours, then frees its slot.
• Time comes from the device clock; a clock set backwards freezes goal timers until it catches up.
Contributing
• Hand in accepted items at the goal's drop-off ports. Each item adds points (1 for most items; rarer items more) toward target_points. Every contributor sees the progress bar, top crews, and time left.
• Every hand-in pays at once: goal_price = max(that port's buy price, base_value) × contribution_premium. This is kept even if the goal expires.
• Items bought at a goal's own drop-off port cannot be handed in, so a crew can't loop buy-and-contribute there. Items bought anywhere else count.
• During the goal, the drop-off port's demand for accepted items rises (a market event), so NPC traders route there too. NPC hand-ins are capped at npc_contribution_cap of the target, so players always do most of the work.
• Milestones at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% send a comms broadcast and a story update to every player.
Bonus
Paid only when the target is met before the deadline. Each contributing crew gets:
\text{bonus} = \min\left(\text{base\_bonus} + \text{per\_point\_bonus} \times \text{points},\ \text{bonus\_cap}\right)
Every contributor gets at least base_bonus, so a crew that brought a few items still shares the win. A met goal also grants a cosmetic (decal or paint), a codex badge, and a 7-day perk in that system (such as 10% off repairs). An expired goal pays no bonus and closes with a gentler epilogue ("Luna pulled through, just barely").
Worth the trip
The importer checks every goal template so that a starter ship can make a profitable trip:
\text{cargo\_units} \times \text{goal\_price} + \text{expected\_bonus\_share} \ \ge\ 1.5 \times (\text{round\_trip\_fuel} + \text{supply\_cost})
The check uses the tier-1 cargo hold, the farthest system within 4 jumps, and buying the full load at the cheapest source port. Goals that fail it are rejected at import. In game, the goal board shows each crew "About +X credits per trip after fuel" for their own ship and location.

Code changes, in order
No code has changed yet. Each step builds on the one before and leaves the game playable.
1.  Content IDs and importer. Add id to every content asset. Add SolarSystem and SpacePort assets and retire the SolarSystemFound and DestinationPort enums. Generalize AIDataImporter into one importer for all content (it currently writes to Assets/Data/AI, not the existing ScriptableObjects/Ship AI Messages folder).
2.  ContentDatabase. One runtime lookup from ID to asset for every content type.
3.  Ship-owned state. Move cargo and credits from the CargoManager and WalletManager singletons onto each ship. Retire the PlayerWallet asset: its value carries over between play sessions in the Editor and resets on every launch in a build. Cargo capacity comes from hull plus Cargo upgrade.
4.  Item instances. Cargo holds ItemInstance records, created on tractor pickup and mining drops. Key HUD icons by instance: today SellSpecificItem removes the first icon whose name matches, and returns before its ItemSold message plays. Replace DestroyImmediate(..., true) in RemoveAllItems with Destroy, since the true flag can delete assets.
5.  Save system. ISaveStorage with the PC implementation, Universe serialization, schema_version.
6.  Markets. PortMarket, the price formula, buying and selling in SpacePortUI, and the buyback rule.
7.  Economy tick and NPC traders. Timer-only NPCs first, then visible NPC ships in a player's system. Market events (such as a festival doubling food demand in one system) ship in this step.
8.  Upgrades. UpgradeDefinition and UpgradeStat replace ShipUpgrade and MiningLaserStats. StoreManager installs and trades in. UpgradeButton finds the next tier by ID, not by comparing asset name strings.
9.  Damage and repairs. Hooks in collisions, LaserController and TractorBeam overheat; performance scaling; port repair service.
10.  Deliveries. Contracts for LivingCargo and passenger chatter.
11.  Split-screen and crew seats. PlayerInputManager joining, one Cinemachine camera per ship, Pilot and Co-pilot seats. SpacePortManager handles one playerObj and one camera today, plus a skip-first-trigger workaround (didIAwake); it needs to handle several ships.
12.  Ship-to-ship docking. DockingSession, offers, gifts, quick chat; then NPC docking.
13.  Dialog. Speakers, channels, tokens, exchanges.
14.  Console saves. Switch and Xbox ISaveStorage implementations, which need their dev kits and SDKs.
15.  Community goals. GoalTemplate import and validation, the local GoalService in the save, the scheduler, the goal board at ports and the HUD progress ticker. Needs markets (step 6), deliveries (step 10) and the save system (step 5). An online GoalService on PlayFab comes later, only if the game goes online.
Open questions
[ ] Quick-chat options for ship-to-ship comms: which preset lines and emotes, and how they're grouped in the menu. The set includes at least: "Any trades?", a request to dock sent to any ship in the same system, "Any good salvage?" and "Any good mining?".
[ ] Does the buyback rule expire after some sim time, or apply forever to that item at that port? Decided: forever.
[ ] Can players buy upgrades at any port with an Upgrade service, or only certain tiers at certain port types? Decided: by the system's industry, through sold_in_industries[]. A Medical system offers only fuel and basic repairs; a Tech system offers the widest range of upgrades.
[ ] Perishables (medical supplies): when one perishes, does it vanish, or become a low-value item? Decided: it becomes a low-value item.
[ ] Do NPC traders also run deliveries, or only trade goods? Decided: they also run deliveries.
[ ] Market events (for example a festival doubling food demand in one system): in the first version, or later? Decided: first version.
[ ] Community goals: shared online by every player of the game (needs a cross-platform backend on PC, Switch and Xbox), or per save, shared by the household playing that universe at different times? Decided: local multiplayer only for now; PlayFab if the game goes online later.
